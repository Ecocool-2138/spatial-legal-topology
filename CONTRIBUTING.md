# Contributing

Spatial Legal Topology is an exploratory legal-informatics project. Contributions from law, jurisprudence, legal informatics, knowledge graphs, software engineering, AI/NLP and visualisation are welcome.

## Core rule

**AI proposes; authorities support; humans verify.**

A generated legal proposition is never treated as verified merely because an AI system produced it.

## Useful contribution types

- verify or challenge a node or edge against primary authority;
- propose a better ontology term;
- model a bounded legal problem;
- improve data schemas or graph queries;
- build visualisations;
- test temporal or jurisdictional modelling;
- document ambiguity, disagreement or failure cases.

## Legal propositions

A contribution asserting a legal relationship should include, where possible:

1. the source authority;
2. a pinpoint paragraph/section;
3. the proposed proposition;
4. its authority status (for example ratio, authoritative dictum, obiter, secondary or proposed);
5. any important conditions, exceptions or competing authorities.

Where reasonable lawyers could disagree, mark the proposition `CONTESTED` rather than forcing false certainty.

## Development workflow

Small, reviewable pull requests are preferred. For unresolved architectural questions, open an Issue (or Discussion where enabled) before writing substantial code.

## Research integrity

Do not fabricate citations, silently paraphrase authorities beyond what they support, or promote an AI-generated relationship to `HUMAN_VERIFIED` without genuine human review.

## Scope

This repository is research/software, not legal advice. Examples are used to test representation and reasoning methods, not to provide conclusions for individual legal disputes.
